Nutrient values come from USDA FoodData Central (Foundation, SR Legacy & Survey/FNDDS data). Vitamin/mineral targets use general adult RDAs - this is a planning tool, not a substitute for a dietitian, especially with kidney, thyroid, or other conditions where potassium/phosphorus intake needs individual attention. Iodine isn't tracked here. B12 and vitamin D are tracked, but vegan whole foods rarely provide them naturally, so keep an eye on those two bars and talk to a doctor about supplementing if they stay low.

*Marks the 9 essential amino acids - ones your body can't make on its own, so they have to come from food. Cystine and Tyrosine are "conditionally essential" instead (the body can make them from Methionine and Phenylalanine respectively, given enough of those), which is why they're unmarked. Also worth knowing: Methionine/Cystine and Phenylalanine/Tyrosine are officially scored by WHO/FAO as combined pairs, not individually - each amino acid in a pair is shown against that same shared target as a simplification, rather than two independently-derived numbers.

Each amino acid row shows two WHO/FAO/UNU-derived scales at once: the number/%/color it's judged against is your body-weight-based physiological floor (a fixed daily minimum, independent of diet), while the bar's full width is scaled to a more generous protein-quality-based reference amount (the same ratio used for PDCAAS/DIAAS scoring, scaled to your protein goal). The red tick mark inside each bar shows exactly where that body-weight floor sits along the bar - hover it for the precise number.
